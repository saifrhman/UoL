\section{Clustering Algorithm: Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering}

Agglomerative Hierarchical Clustering (AHC) is a bottom-up clustering
algorithm that builds a hierarchy of nested partitions by iteratively merging
the two closest clusters.  The process begins with $N$ singleton clusters ---
one per data point --- and terminates when all points belong to a single
cluster.  A flat partition into $K$ clusters is obtained by cutting the
resulting dendrogram at the level that produces $K$ groups.  The cut point
is selected by maximising the silhouette coefficient over $K = 1, \ldots, 10$.

\subsection{Algorithm Mechanics}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Single linkage:}
        $d(A,B) = \min_{a \in A,\, b \in B} d(a,b)$.
        Prone to chaining; produces elongated clusters.

  \item \textbf{Average linkage:}
        $d(A,B) = \frac{1}{|A||B|}\sum_{a \in A}\sum_{b \in B} d(a,b)$.
        Less prone to chaining but susceptible on imbalanced corpora.

  \item \textbf{Ward linkage (used in this work):}  merges the pair $(A, B)$
        minimising the increase in total within-cluster sum of squares.
        Produces compact, balanced, well-separated clusters.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why AHC?}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No assumption about cluster shape.}  Unlike $K$-means, AHC
        recovers clusters of arbitrary shape and size, accommodating the
        strong size imbalance in this dataset (Shakespeare $\approx 46\%$,
        modern English $\approx 49\%$, Notre Dame sub-corpora $<5\%$).

  \item \textbf{Deterministic output.}  AHC produces the same result on
        every run, unlike $K$-means, which depends on random centroid
        initialisation.

  \item \textbf{No need to pre-specify $K$.}  AHC builds the complete
        dendrogram in a single pass; the optimal $K$ is selected post-hoc by
        silhouette analysis.

  \item \textbf{Literature support.}  AHC is a well-validated algorithm for
        text clustering with dense embeddings (Petukhova et al., 2024).
\end{itemize}

\section{Configuration: Ward Linkage and Euclidean Distance}

\subsection{Ward Linkage}

Ward linkage (Ward, 1963) selects the merge that minimises the increase in
total within-cluster sum of squares (WCSS):

\begin{mathbox}
\[
  \Delta(A,\,B)
  \;=\;
  \frac{|A|\,|B|}{|A|+|B|}
  \;\|\boldsymbol{\mu}_A - \boldsymbol{\mu}_B\|^{2}
\]
\end{mathbox}

Ward is the only linkage criterion that directly optimises cluster compactness
at every merge step, and is robust against the chaining problem.

\subsection{Euclidean Distance}

Ward linkage is mathematically defined \emph{only} for Euclidean distance.
The feature matrix passed to AHC is the 5-dimensional UMAP output, which lies
in an unconstrained Euclidean space.  Euclidean distance in this space is
therefore both the correct and the only valid metric for Ward-linkage AHC.

Furthermore, L2 normalisation was applied to the raw embeddings before PCA,
and UMAP's neighbourhood graph was built using cosine distance.  For unit
vectors, Euclidean and cosine distances are monotonically equivalent:

\begin{mathbox}
\[
  d_{\mathrm{Euclidean}}(\hat{\mathbf{a}},\,\hat{\mathbf{b}})^{2}
  = 2\bigl(1 - \cos(\hat{\mathbf{a}},\,\hat{\mathbf{b}})\bigr)
\]
\end{mathbox}

This ensures that Euclidean distances in the UMAP output space remain
consistent with the cosine-based semantic geometry of the embedding model.

\section{Silhouette Analysis}

The silhouette coefficient is an internal cluster validity index
(Rousseeuw, 1987):

\begin{mathbox}
\begin{align*}
  a(i) &= \text{mean intra-cluster Euclidean distance from } i \\[4pt]
  b(i) &= \text{mean Euclidean distance from } i \text{ to the nearest other cluster} \\[4pt]
  s(i) &= \frac{b(i) - a(i)}{\max\bigl(a(i),\,b(i)\bigr)}
           \;\in\; [-1,\, +1]
\end{align*}
\end{mathbox}

\begin{mathbox}
\[
  S \;=\; \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} s(i)
\]
\end{mathbox}

Silhouette is computed using Euclidean distance in the same 5-dimensional UMAP
space as clustering --- the only self-consistent choice.  Per recent literature
(PMC, 2025): $S > 0.70$ = strong, $0.50$--$0.70$ = acceptable,
$0.25$--$0.50$ = weak.

\section{Optimal Value of $K$ and Justification}

\subsection{Quantitative Justification: Silhouette Analysis}

The silhouette analysis identifies $\mathbf{K=4}$ as the optimal number of
clusters, with a peak coefficient of $\mathbf{0.758182}$.  This score lies
firmly in the ``strong'' range ($S > 0.70$; PMC, 2025).

The curve has a clear structural signature.  From $K=2$ to $K=6$, scores
remain close together in the range $[0.742, 0.758]$, indicating that several
valid partitions exist in this region.  $K=4$ achieves the peak.  At $K=7$
there is a sharp drop to $0.571$, followed by a further collapse to $0.352$
at $K=8$.  This cliff-edge pattern is a strong quantitative signal: $K \leq 6$
corresponds to genuine thematic structure in the data, while $K \geq 7$ begins
fracturing coherent clusters arbitrarily.  The peak at $K=4$ identifies the
most compact and well-separated partition within this valid range.

\subsection{Cluster-by-Cluster Analysis}

\textbf{Cluster 3 --- Shakespeare (811 documents).}  The largest cluster
captures all Shakespearean dialogue from Coriolanus.  Its separation from
every other cluster is total: Early Modern English vocabulary, syntactic
structures (e.g., inverted word order, subjunctive mood), and the character
prefix format are unique in the dataset.  \texttt{all-mpnet-base-v2}
embeddings place Shakespearean text in a region of $\mathbb{R}^{768}$ that is
maximally distant from any contemporary prose.

\textbf{Cluster 2 --- Contemporary English Prose (861 documents).}  The
second-largest cluster contains informal restaurant and service reviews
(Yelp-style) alongside formal news reports (CNN-style).  Despite differences
in formality, both sub-corpora share modern vocabulary, contemporary syntax,
and prose sentence structure --- the features that the embedding model weighs
most heavily.  The cluster boundary between contemporary reviews and news is
insufficient to form a separable geometric boundary in the UMAP space,
consistent with the observation that only 2 CNN sentences exist in the dataset.

\textbf{Clusters 1 and 4 --- Notre Dame (73 + 15 documents).}  The Wikipedia
Notre Dame sub-corpus (80 rows) is detected as two distinct clusters.  Cluster
1 contains the general institutional content (student organisations, media,
campus history), while Cluster 4 contains exclusively the sentences describing
religious and iconic architectural landmarks (Gold Dome, Grotto, Basilica,
Marian statues).  This split reflects a genuine semantic boundary within the
Notre Dame corpus: the landmark sentences form a highly cohesive, repetitive
group (each of the 3 unique sentences in Cluster 4 appears 5 times) whose
local UMAP neighbourhood is almost entirely self-referential, producing a
tighter, more isolated manifold region than the broader general institutional
content of Cluster 1.

\subsection{Role of Duplicate Retention}

The Notre Dame sub-corpus consists of 18 unique sentences, each repeated 4--5
times (80 rows total).  Without these repetitions, the 18 unique sentences
would represent only 1.1\% of the data.  At \texttt{n\_neighbors=10}, each
Notre Dame sentence would not find enough Notre Dame neighbours to form a
cohesive sub-graph, and the sub-corpus would dissolve into the broader modern
English cluster.

By retaining duplicates, the neighbourhood graph of each Notre Dame sentence
is dominated by other Notre Dame sentences.  UMAP projects this dense,
self-referential sub-graph into a compact, isolated region of the
5-dimensional output space.  Ward-linkage AHC then cleanly separates this
region from the surrounding modern English manifold.  The cluster boundary
is robust: the silhouette score at $K=4$ ($0.758$) exceeds that at $K=3$
($0.743$), confirming that the Notre Dame cluster is genuinely well-separated
and not an artefact.

\subsection{Contextualisation Against the Literature}

Silhouette scores computed in a well-formed 5-dimensional UMAP space (BERTopic
standard: \texttt{metric='cosine'}, \texttt{min\_dist=0.0},
\texttt{n\_components=5}; Grootendorst, 2022) are structurally higher than
scores computed in raw embedding or PCA space, because UMAP sharpens cluster
boundaries in the projected space.  The score of $0.758182$ at $K=4$ is
self-consistent and directly meaningful as a measure of cluster separation
in the representation the algorithm operated on, and falls in the ``strong''
range of the current literature (PMC, 2025).

\section{Additional Considerations}

\subsection{Duplicate Handling}

All 1{,}760 rows --- including the 64 duplicate sentences --- are retained
throughout preprocessing, embedding, and clustering.  \texttt{label.txt}
therefore contains exactly 1{,}760 entries, one per original dataset row.

\subsection{Embedding Model}

\texttt{all-mpnet-base-v2} (768-dim, 110\,M parameters, 1\,B training pairs)
is the highest-quality standard sentence transformer on the Sentence Embeddings
Benchmark.  It runs fully locally with no cloud API dependency.  Petukhova et
al.\ (2024) confirm that BERT-family models represent the best open-source
option for text clustering.
